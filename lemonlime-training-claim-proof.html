Public trust-surface audit / synthetic proof

One promise. Three surfaces. One clearer data boundary.

A compact, non-invasive review of LemonLime's public About, Privacy, and Terms language. The issue is not proof of unsafe processing; it is that an absolute marketing promise sits beside materially qualified policy language.

LemonLime / Claim Alignment
About page

Absolute buyer-facing promise

"LemonLime never trains models on your data."

↓

Easy to understand, but broad enough to sound like it covers LemonLime and every routed model provider.

Privacy policy

Qualified operational boundary

Third-party provider training is governed by that provider's terms and defaults; no-training settings are "generally" configured where available.

↓

This creates a real exception boundary that the About claim does not surface.

Recommended fix

Precise, still concise

"LemonLime does not train its models on Customer Data. For routed third-party models, controls depend on the selected provider and configuration."

↓

Add a direct link to the provider matrix and configuration controls.

Proposed production copy

Keep the promise strong without making it broader than the system.

LemonLime does not use Customer Data to train, fine-tune, or improve LemonLime-operated or shared models. When a customer selects a third-party model, that provider's enterprise terms and configured controls determine retention and training behavior. Customers can restrict eligible providers before data is routed.

This proof uses only public pages and does not assert that any provider currently trains on LemonLime data.
